Alongside the actual building of our first DBMS project, there was a separate requirement that turned out to be its own small challenge: recording a video explaining our project properly. This was not something we could treat as an afterthought. We genuinely had to sit down and explain our work clearly, in a way that someone watching it could actually follow what we had built and why we had built it that way.

What made this particular requirement a bit more involved was the format it needed to be in. We were asked to produce the video in B-form, which meant we could not just record something casually and submit it as is. There was a specific structure expected, and getting that right took some extra attention on top of everything else we were already juggling with the project itself.

Once the video was actually finished, the next step was uploading it to LinkedIn. This added a slightly different dimension to the whole exercise, since it meant our explanation was not just being submitted privately for evaluation, but was also going up somewhere more public and professional. Knowing that, we tried to be a bit more deliberate about how we presented ourselves and our work in the recording, since this was not just an internal assignment anymore.

Within the video itself, we also had to show our environment during the DBMS exhibition, which meant the video was not purely a talking-through-the-project kind of recording. It had to actually capture some of the atmosphere and setting of the exhibition itself, giving anyone watching a sense of what the event looked and felt like, not just a description of the technical work behind it.

On top of recording and showing our environment, we were also expected to give faculty reviews as part of this same process. This meant reflecting, on camera, on the feedback and input we had received from faculty members, which added another layer of honesty to the video beyond just explaining our own project. It required us to think about our experience from the outside as well, considering how our work had been received and reviewed rather than only describing what we had built.

This particular exhibition, unlike some of our earlier ones, took place in our department's computer lab. There was something fitting about that setting, given how technical the project itself was. Presenting database work and a trained model in the same lab environment where so much of the actual coding and testing had originally happened gave the whole event a kind of continuity that I appreciated more in hindsight than I necessarily noticed at the time.

Looking back at this entire video and exhibition requirement, I think it taught me something slightly different from the project itself. The database design and model training tested my technical understanding, but the video, the B-form requirement, and the public LinkedIn upload tested something closer to communication and presentation, skills that matter just as much in this field but rarely get the same attention as the purely technical side of a project.
