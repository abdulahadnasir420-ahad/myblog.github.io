If there is one thing I learned from the DBMS final exam, it is that you can prepare with a fairly clear expectation of what is coming and still end up facing something quite different once the paper is actually in front of you. Going into the exam, our instructor had been steering us toward focusing more heavily on relational algebra and anomalies rather than on writing SQL queries directly, and he made a genuine effort to push us in that direction during the lead-up to the exam.

Despite that, most of us as students had built up a different expectation among ourselves, largely assuming that SQL queries would still make up a significant part of the actual paper. I think this is a fairly natural thing that happens before exams, where the plan an instructor has communicated and the expectation students quietly build among themselves do not always line up perfectly, even when the signals pointing toward one outcome are fairly clear in hindsight.

When the exam finally arrived, it confirmed exactly what had been emphasized rather than what most of us had been quietly expecting. The paper was based on relational algebra and anomalies, just as had been indicated beforehand, with SQL queries not showing up the way many of us had assumed they would. I remember a brief moment of recalibration once I saw the paper, having to mentally shift away from the SQL-focused preparation I had partly leaned on and rely instead on the relational algebra and anomaly concepts that, fortunately, had also been part of our preparation all along.

This experience taught me something about how to actually listen to the signals an instructor gives before an exam, rather than letting assumptions built up informally among classmates override clearer guidance that has already been communicated directly. It is an easy trap to fall into, assuming a paper will look a certain way simply because that is the pattern you are used to, rather than paying close attention to what has actually been emphasized in the lead-up to the exam itself.

Alongside the final exam, there was still the matter of lab manual submissions, which followed the same pattern we had used earlier in the semester. We submitted them with the same ring-binding format as before, keeping things consistent with how we had already been organizing this kind of work. The marks attached to this part of the course were tied directly to the lab component, which meant getting the manuals submitted properly was not a minor formality but an actual contributor to our final standing in the subject.

With the final exam completed and the lab manuals submitted, my second semester came to a close. Looking back at it now, this semester ended the same way it had largely unfolded throughout: full of new experiences and a fair number of genuine difficulties, from project work and exhibitions to exam expectations that did not quite match what we had assumed, all layered on top of the usual demands of coursework and deadlines. It was not a simple stretch of time, but it was one that, looking back, taught me a great deal about adapting to circumstances as they actually unfolded rather than as I had originally pictured them.

This brings my second semester story to a close. Between Programming Fundamentals in my first semester and Database Management Systems in my second, this first year has been full of difficult starts, genuine setbacks, and slow but real improvement, and I am carrying all of it forward into whatever comes next in this degree.
