On the 15th of September, I walked through the gates of UET's Faisalabad campus for the very first time, and I will be honest, I was nervous in a way I hadn't expected. It wasn't the ordinary first-day jitters everyone talks about. It was the feeling of stepping into a completely new chapter of life, one that had no familiar landmarks at all. I had just been admitted into Computer Engineering, a field I had chosen with excitement but also with a fair share of uncertainty about what the next four years would actually look like.

The first real test of that uncertainty came before I even set foot in a classroom. I moved into the hostel, and that alone was an adjustment I hadn't fully prepared for. I had never lived in a government hostel before, and the experience felt strange in those first few days. The rhythm of hostel life is nothing like home. There's no one reminding you to eat on time, no familiar voice checking in on your day, and the silence of an unfamiliar room at night has a way of making you reflect on just how big a change you've taken on. But strange doesn't mean bad, and slowly, almost without me noticing, the discomfort started to fade into something more manageable. By the end of the first week, I felt comfortable enough to actually focus on why I was there in the first place: to study and to grow.

The next big moment was orientation day, and then my first actual walk to a university classroom. I remember that walk vividly because it was loaded with a kind of anticipation I hadn't felt since the day I got my admission letter. I had never seen a university classroom before, not really, and I had never walked through a campus that wasn't a school. Everything about it felt larger than life. When I finally reached the classroom and took it in, a wave of mild anxiety hit me. What was going to happen next? Would I understand anything? Would I fit in? These are the kinds of questions that swirl in your head when everything around you is unfamiliar.

As it turned out, that first day didn't actually involve any real classes. It was mostly formalities, introductions, and getting a feel for the campus layout. But even that was enough to plant the realization that real classes were about to begin, and there was no turning back from this new identity I was stepping into as an engineering student.

Looking back now, I think what made that first day memorable wasn't anything dramatic that happened. It was simply the *feeling* of it: the nervous energy, the unfamiliarity of the hostel, the first glimpse of a university classroom, all combining into a moment that marked the actual start of my engineering journey. Every student who has gone through a transition like this, leaving home for the first time and stepping into a professional academic environment, probably remembers their own version of this day. Mine was set against the backdrop of UET Faisalabad's Computer Engineering department, a place that would go on to shape the next few years of my life in ways I couldn't have predicted on that first nervous walk to class.

From the very next day onward, the actual rhythm of university life began. Classes started according to a proper schedule, and with them came the subject that would define my entire first semester: Programming Fundamentals. But that story belongs to the next chapter of this journey.

There's a particular kind of loneliness that comes with those first few nights in a hostel room, even when the room itself isn't uncomfortable and even when you know, rationally, that thousands of other students have gone through the exact same adjustment before you. It's not really about the physical space. It's about the absence of every small, automatic comfort that home provides without you ever having to think about it: familiar food, familiar voices, a routine you didn't have to build from scratch. None of that exists on day one in a new hostel, and the adjustment period is really just the slow process of building a new version of that comfort, piece by piece, until the room stops feeling like someone else's space and starts feeling like yours.

I remember spending a good portion of those early evenings just sitting and thinking about whether I'd made the right choice, whether Computer Engineering was really where I belonged, whether the version of university life I'd imagined matched the one I was actually stepping into. Those questions don't really get answered in a day, or even in a week. They get answered slowly, through accumulated small experiences: a helpful comment from a senior, a class that goes better than expected, a professor who explains something in a way that finally makes it click. The first day doesn't answer any of those questions. It just opens the door to a process that will.

If you're a new student reading this, especially one moving into a hostel or a new city for the first time, I'd tell you exactly what my seniors told me later: the discomfort of the first few days is temporary, and it eventually turns into one of the memories you look back on fondly. Hang in there. It gets better, and more familiar, faster than you think. And on the days when it doesn't feel that way yet, it's worth remembering that almost everyone around you, classmates and seniors alike, went through some version of this exact same uncertainty before they found their footing.
