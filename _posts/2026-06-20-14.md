Our second and final DBMS project took a noticeably different shape from the first one. Rather than another round of dataset selection and model building, this submission, taken from us after our exams had finished, asked us to describe our own journey, and to do it across fifteen posts rather than as a single piece of writing.

This was an interesting shift in what was being asked of us. Up to that point, most of our DBMS work had been technical: databases, models, frontends, exhibitions. This final project asked for something more reflective, putting our own semester into words across a structured set of posts rather than a single report or presentation. I found this format genuinely useful for thinking back over everything that had happened, since trying to compress an entire semester into one piece of writing tends to flatten a lot of detail, while spreading it across fifteen separate posts gave room to actually sit with individual moments rather than rushing past them.

Beyond just describing the semester itself, the project also asked us to describe whatever circumstances came up around us during that same period, which meant the submission was not purely about academics. It was about the fuller context surrounding our coursework, the things happening in our lives alongside the technical work we were doing.

What made the timing of this particular submission memorable was that it landed right around the Muharram-ul-Haram holidays. Under normal circumstances, this would have meant a natural pause in academic deadlines, but given how this submission had been scheduled, our instructor still expected us to complete and turn in our work despite the holiday period overlapping with it. I will be honest that this added a layer of pressure to an already demanding stretch of the semester, since balancing the observance of Muharram alongside an active submission deadline was not something I had anticipated needing to manage.

Even with that added difficulty, we made a genuine effort to get everything submitted properly. It would have been easy to let the holiday period become an excuse to fall behind, but we tried to treat the deadline seriously regardless of the timing, putting in the effort needed to finish the fifteen posts and submit them as required, even while the rest of our routine during that period looked different from a normal academic week.

Looking back, I think this project ended up teaching me something slightly different from the rest of my DBMS coursework. The technical projects taught me about databases and models. This final submission taught me about discipline under timing pressure that had nothing to do with the subject matter itself, the kind of pressure that comes from real life simply not pausing to accommodate an academic deadline, and the need to manage both responsibly rather than letting one fully override the other.

In the next post, I want to talk about the DBMS final exam itself, and the way our actual exam content ended up differing quite a bit from what most of us had expected and prepared for.
